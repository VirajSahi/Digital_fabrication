I measured this using the laboratory DC power supply, tracking the increase in current against increased weight/pressure on the sensor. <br><br> During assembly, I struggled with the copper tape’s strong adhesive, which blocked conductivity when placed on the velostat. My solution was to lay the copper tape with the non-sticky side down and then secure it in place with cloth tape. Since the ouput on the serial monitor was in arbitrary units rather than kilograms, I ran a program to find an appropriate calibration factor. I started with the container empty, then placed known weights inside (using varying volumes of water). I monitored the change in readings and adjusted the calibration factor in real time by pressing + or - in the Serial Monitor until the output matched the known weight. Below is the calibration code: </h5> <pre style="font-size: 16px;"><code class="cpp"> #include "HX711.h" #define DOUT 4 #define CLK 5 HX711 scale; float calibration_factor = -17400; void setup() { Serial.begin(9600); Serial.println("HX711 calibration sketch"); Serial.println("Remove all weight from scale"); Serial.println("After readings begin, place known weight on scale"); Serial.println("Press + or a to increase calibration factor"); Serial.println("Press - or z to decrease calibration factor"); scale.begin(DOUT, CLK); scale.set_scale(); scale.tare(); long zero_factor = scale.read_average(); Serial.print("Zero factor: "); Serial.println(zero_factor); } void loop() { scale.set_scale(calibration_factor); Serial.print("Reading: "); Serial.print(scale.get_units(), 3); Serial.print(" kg"); Serial.print(" calibration_factor: "); Serial.print(calibration_factor); Serial.println(); if(Serial.available()) { char temp = Serial.read(); if(temp == '+' || temp == 'a') calibration_factor += 10; else if(temp == '-' || temp == 'z') calibration_factor -= 10; } } </code></pre><br> <h5> I then verified my calibration factor by testing volumes of water from 0 to 64 fluid ounces, moving up by increments of 4 oz., then jumping to 124 oz., and sampling 13 values at each instance. After the first try, I had to recalibrate from -19520 to -19040, yet the second try was far more successful. Below are photos of my values table, and 2 graphs: the first plotting my measured values against the projected/expected values for each volume (1 fluid oz. of water is 0.118 kg), and the second plotting the line of best fit for measured values. The best fit turned out to be y=0.119x, which is 0.001 away from the expected value!
