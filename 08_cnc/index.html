<div class="textcontainer"> <p class="margin"> </p> <h3>Week 8: CNC Milling</h3> <h4>Assignment: Make Something With CNC</h4><br> <h4 style="text-align:center"><u>Intro</u><h4><br> <h5>This week's assignment was a pleasant break from the intense, coding-heavy assignments of the past few weeks. Using the new techniques we learned, I made 5 objects:</h5> <ul> <li>A plaster casting of my <b>finger</b></li> <li>An <b>insole</b> (orthotic) for my foot</b></li> <li>A bismuth <b>bobby pin</b> to tie back hair</b></li> <li>A <b>chocolate mold</b> of Bobby's face</li> <li>A <b>topographic map</b> of my hometown</li> </ul> <h4 style="text-align:center"><u>Finger cast</u></h4><br> <h5> One of the first objects I created was a plaster casting of my thumb, using Alja-safe for the mold. Rather than being an end in and of itself, the finger was just a way for me to try out the new technique so that I could implement it on a larger scale with my custom insole. Here are photos of the result: </h5><br> <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px; align-items: stretch;"> <img src="Thumbcast2.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 450px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> <img src="Thumbcast1.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 450px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> </div><br><br> <h4 style="text-align:center"><u>Insoles</u></h4><br> <h5> Next, I created insoles (also known as orthotics). I'm flat footed, and getting affordable arch supports that fit my feet was always difficult as a child. I wanted to make custom arch supports. First, I created modeled and laser-cut a box that fit my foot snugly, in which I could pour the alja-safe without wasting a lot of material. Next, I pressed my foot into the material to capture its shape, then cast plaster into the negative: <i>(apologies for the somewhat distasteful foot pic):</i> </h5><br> <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px; align-items: stretch;"> <img src="Footmold1.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 340px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> <img src="FootBoxDXF.png" alt="Description" style="height: 340px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> </div><br> <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px; align-items: stretch;"> <img src="Footmold4.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 420px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> <img src="Footmold2.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 420px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> <img src="Footmold3.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 420px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> </div><br> <h5> Afterwards, I vacuum formed plastic around the mold. I wasn't sure how this would turn out, as the plaster was very brittle. Furthermore, my foot cast didn't fit inside the vacuum forming machine, so I had to snap off the toes. I vaccum formed it thrice: each time, I added the previous plastic form on the original mold to offeset the volume. Finally I joint all 3 together with epoxy. </h5><br> <div style="display: flex; gap: 10px; align-items: stretch;"> <img src="Footmold6.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 380px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> <img src="Footmold7.jpg" alt="Description" style="height: 380px; width: auto; object-fit: contain;"/> </div><br> <h5> Next, I create a composite out of burlap and epoxy to reinforce the structure while providing some flexibility. I attached this to the vacuum-formed plastic base. Afterwards, I added foam buildups to the arch region to support the curve of my foot. Finally, glued one, large a layer of foam on top to provide comfort. It ended up fitting ectremely comfortably!!! My aim was to make a bobby pin with bobby's face on it (I promise, this seemed incredibly funny at the time). I beganned by scanning Bobby's lovely head using the RevoPoint scanner, then creating the pin on fusion. This took over 2 hours, as the software had trouble processing the many faces. Then I CNC-milled the design onto a block of wax. I wasn't able to complete the finishing pass, so I chiselled the inner curve of the pin by hand. Then I cast a negative of the pin by pouring silicone into the wax mold.
